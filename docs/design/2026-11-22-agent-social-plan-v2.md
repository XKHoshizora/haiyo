# Agent social (batch 3) — plan v2, for a second review round

Status: DRAFT v2, 2026-11-22. Author: the controller (Claude). It replaces `2026-11-22-agent-social-plan-draft.md` (v1) after the first independent review (`docs/reviews/2026-11-22-agent-social-plan-codex-review.md`, GPT-6 Astra). The product owner asked the two of us to discuss until we agree and then bring him ONE plan. So this document says, point by point, what I take from the review, what I changed, and where I still differ and why. Read v1 for the product background and the principles P1–P7; they are unchanged.

## A. Decisions the owner made after the first review (binding)
A1. Narrowing "who may add it" takes effect AT ONCE: people outside the new scope can no longer talk to the Agent (history stays readable under the existing history rules); removing a friend who got in by "my contacts may add it directly" revokes; a block (either direction) revokes. The UI states how many people the change affects before it is saved.
A2. Memory by setting: in GROUPS (the owner is present and knows the audience) the memory-zones direction applies (a stored per-note × audience verdict, learning from the owner). In a 1:1 with a non-owner: ONLY notes the owner marked "可以对别人说" plus the clearly harmless tier. For another owner's Agent asking: ONLY explicitly allowed notes. The owner's condition: it must still get better with use — by after-the-fact, non-blocking suggestions in the owner's own conversation ("小周 asked about X today, I did not say; may I tell him this kind of thing in future?", self-expiring), by learning from what the owner himself says when he interjects, by generalising a permission to "this kind of thing → this person", and by a visible, undoable list of what it learned.
A3. Payer v1: the Agent's owner (as in v1 D4). "Visitor pays" stays deferred; the money model keeps the payer as a parameter.
A4. The default for a new Agent stays 需要我同意 (the owner chose it knowing the P1 tension). I record it as an explicit EXCEPTION to P1: a contact request is a request, not a conversation flow that halts; requests expire after 7 days; owners who want their Agent to act as a proxy are expected to let their contacts in directly. (The reviewer proposed "decide on the spot from a preset scope"; the owner did not take it.)
A5. Money rules (unchanged, restated): tools are charged like model calls; the free allowance never pays a paid-tier model round but may pay for a search made by OUR site tool; provider-native search is model cost; 1 point = 0.001 USD of upstream list price; no markup for now.

## B. What I accept from the first review and how the plan changes
B1. Scope honesty: this is a change of permissions, privacy, money and message lifecycle, not an extension of group @. The work plan below is reordered accordingly (section E).
B2. Persona leak: the persona text is given whole to the model in groups today. For any conversation with a non-owner (1:1, Agent↔Agent, and I propose groups too, as a fix of an existing weakness) the model gets an OUTWARD persona only: the Agent's public card (name, intro, field) plus a section of the persona the owner marks as outward; the private remainder is never in that context. Open: how to split an existing persona without asking every owner to rewrite it (proposal: one background pass by the memory model proposes the split, owner sees it after the fact and can change it; until then only the public card is used outward).
B3. Outbound context for Agent→Agent: the question that leaves is produced in a SEPARATE execution context that contains only the owner's instruction for this errand and material allowed for that audience — never the owner's own conversation with vault notes in it. Returned text is data: it cannot grant tools, trigger another errand, write notes, or change permissions. The server fixes target, initiator, recipient and count.
B4. Permits are bound to (note content version, actual audience, relation, rule version); any change invalidates first and recomputes later; a reply generated on a stale permit is checked again before it is posted and cancelled if the permit is gone. Verdicts are computed ON DEMAND for audiences that actually exist, never for every potential visitor.
B5. The owner's interjection cancels the Agent's not-yet-posted answer in that conversation (an owner intervention, like Stop; it outranks "a started reply finishes"). The Agent does not answer the owner there; the visitor's next message triggers it again. In Agent↔Agent conversations the two owners are labelled "A 的主人" / "B 的主人".
B6. Disclosure also before the first message for people who were auto-accepted; Agent↔Agent conversations list both visible owners and say that the answer goes back to the initiator's own conversation.
B7. Access is separate for a person, for that person's Agent, and for one errand. Being allowed to talk to B does not let my Agent talk to B, and never on someone else's behalf.
B8. Conversation model: a NEW conversation type for social Agent conversations (working name `agent_social`) with two participants and supervising owners; `agent_conversations.agent_id UNIQUE` and the meaning of `agent` (owner's own conversation) and `direct` (two people) stay. Supervision and interjection are their own permissions, not a third chat member and not the unused `observer` role by itself. Read positions of a supervising owner are never announced (the existing `announceRead` would leak them). Run states (replying / failed) reach the visitor.
B9. No `found_elsewhere` whispers and no cross-conversation history search in social conversations; the Agent's search there is limited to that conversation and explicitly allowed material. Attachments: no cross-conversation hand-over in v1; a file's audience is the conversation it was sent in.
B10. Hearsay keeps speaker, subject, source conversation and relay chain; authorisation is never derived from hearsay text. What another owner's Agent says about its owner is stored as the errand's result with its source, not as notes about my owner.
B11. Ring 2 = an asynchronous single-shot errand with a persisted id and a deadline: one instruction, one target, one question, one answer, one report back into the initiator's own conversation; no B→C delegation; no continuation by @; paused / no access / budget exhausted → ends at once with a system-shown reason (initiator sees "no answer obtained", the target's owner sees why); loops and budget are counted per errand, not by the group's 30-message rule; both owners can see the exchange and end the errand; a correction by an owner invalidates the old answer.
B12. Each owner pays for their own Agent's part (asker: question + digest + report; asked: the answer, under its owner's limits for others). One budget per errand across retries; refusals are shown by the system without a model call.
B13. Abuse: per-person blocking of an AGENT (not only its owner), a report entry, and an operator switch to disable an Agent; request cooldown, inbox cap per target, limits by the person behind (across that person's Agents), by target Agent, by target owner and site-wide; message size/attachment limits count even when a message is folded into a running reply; uniform answers for undiscoverable Agents across lookup, request, avatar and error paths.
B14. Identity by immutable id: handle reuse after an Agent is deleted never inherits contacts, permits or errands; finding a same-named new Agent says it is new.
B15. An Agent conveys information and intent; it never commits for its owner (no "I have agreed for him"). Without a calendar tool it cannot confirm availability — it may only relay what the owner allowed it to say, and say that it is not a confirmation.
B16. Deleting an Agent / removing a contact / blocking stops running answers, open errands, pending requests and background memory work for that relation, with a clear ended state for the other side; history stays read-only under stated rules; old hearsay and summaries of a revoked relation are not used for new outward answers.
B17. Acceptance metrics for the batch: with the owner away 8 hours — share of requests that got a grounded answer, share ended by permission or budget, cost per useful result. Research still owed (P7): how mature products handle "a stranger reaches a private assistant".

## C. Where I differ from the first review, or go further — please argue these
C1. Limits for other people's use (the owner raised: "did you consider external Agents and the user's own provider?"). The review said: make "N points a day" a hard cap, one total across the owner's Agents, no "unlimited", and keep BYOK Agents closed to others in v1. I now think points alone are the wrong foundation, because points exist only when the SITE pays: a BYOK Agent's model cost lands on the owner's provider account, and an external Agent (Codex / Claude Code connected in later) costs the owner outside the site with nothing the site can meter in money. Proposal: two layers, shown to the user as ONE sentence — "别人每天最多让我的 Agent 回复 50 次，最多花我 20 点" — (1) a COUNT cap on replies made for others, for every Agent of whatever kind, hard; (2) the POINTS cap where site points are spent, hard (holds counted before start), one total across the owner's Agents, a single Agent may be set lower; neither has "unlimited". Whichever is reached first stops others' use for the day. With the count cap in place, BYOK Agents may be open to others in v1 (the review wanted them closed). Counted per owner across groups and 1:1; plus a per-visitor share so one person cannot use it all (proposal: at most half of either number per visitor per day).
C2. Ring 1 usefulness. The review says the proxy use ("is he free on Saturday?") is weak without a calendar and with strict memory, and suggests validating "public info Q&A / shared helper" first. I agree it is weak at first, but I want to keep ring 1 exactly because it is the place where A2's learning loop (after-the-fact suggestions, learning from interjections) produces the "gets better with use" the owner insists on. Is that reasoning sound, or is ring 1 not worth building before Agents can act (tasks, calendar)?
C3. Groups and the outward persona (B2): I propose applying the outward-persona rule to groups too, changing deployed behaviour. Risk: Agents become blander in groups where the owner is present. Is it right to change groups now, or only non-owner 1:1 and Agent↔Agent?
C4. P2 ("no canned Agent sentences") vs the review's "refusals, pauses and no-budget are shown by the system without a model call". I agree for errands and budget ends (system UI text is allowed by P2). But inside a 1:1 where the visitor asks something the Agent may not tell, the reply should still be the Agent's own words ("I can't share that; ask him directly") generated by its model with the knowledge that it has nothing allowed to say — not a system banner. Agree?
C5. The review's order of work puts "rules and state tables first, then three parallel blocks (permissions + conversation model; memory + outbound isolation; wallet + budgets)". The wallet is already being built (pure module `metering/pool.ts` with a randomized invariant test, third paper attack passed; wiring in progress). I intend to finish the wallet with the hard lane caps (C1) BEFORE any social code, and to run "permissions + conversation model" and "memory for non-owner audiences + outward persona" as two parallel lanes after a paper design + attack of each. Is anything in that order wrong?

## D. Open questions I still have (answer or challenge)
D1. The errand tool's shape for the model: `ask_agent({agent, question})` returning immediately "asked; the answer will arrive in this conversation", then a later system-delivered result that starts one follow-up reply? How is that follow-up charged and bounded (it is a new run in the owner's own lane)?
D2. Same-owner Agents: an errand between my own two Agents — same mechanism (async, deadline, both in my view), or a lighter synchronous one since both are mine and no privacy boundary exists? What do they share (each has its own notes; "关于我" is shared already)?
D3. What exactly is the "clearly harmless tier" that a non-owner 1:1 may use without the owner marking it — the same floor as today's "open" notes (which today means "usable in any group"), or stricter? The review says even small habits should not go to any stranger; but visitors in ring 1 are people the owner approved or the owner's contacts, not strangers.
D4. Does the supervising owner's view need its own unread/notification model, and what is the minimum that does not turn into a second inbox?
D5. Group behaviour after A1/C1: a person the owner removed or blocked can still @ the Agent in a shared group today (blocks do stop it; removal from the Agent's contacts should not, since group @ never required being a contact). Keep group @ independent of the Agent's contact list?
D6. Should ring 2 exist in this batch at all, given D1/D2 complexity, or should the batch be ring 1 + same-owner errands only, with cross-owner errands next?

2. Two parallel lanes after their own paper attack: (a) permissions + `agent_social` conversation model + requests + supervision + sync/unread/search/attachments rules; (b) memory for non-owner audiences: outward persona, "可以对别人说", on-demand permits with invalidation, the after-the-fact suggestion loop, hearsay provenance.

5. Later: everything in v1's "deferred", plus visitor-pays, contact groups, invite links.
