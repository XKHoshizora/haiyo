# Agent social (batch 3) — plan v3 (candidate joint plan)

Status: v3 FINAL CANDIDATE, 2026-11-22 (round-3 changes applied; items marked 待负责人确认 are joint recommendations, not decisions). Author: the controller (Claude), after two review rounds with an independent reviewer (GPT-6 Astra via Codex): `docs/reviews/2026-11-22-agent-social-plan-codex-review.md` (round 1) and `…-codex-round2.md` (round 2). v1 holds the product background and principles P1–P7; v2 holds the item-by-item discussion. This document is meant to be the ONE plan both of us stand behind, to be shown to the product owner. Section 9 says how each round-2 disagreement was settled.

## 1. What this batch is
People other than the owner can reach an owner's Agent; the owner always sees and may step in; the Agent tells others only what it is allowed to; the cost to the owner is bounded by limits that hold for every kind of Agent. Then: an owner can send one of their Agents on a single errand to another of their own Agents. Cross-owner errands in a later, separate release is the joint recommendation (待负责人确认, question 1).

Why so small: today's Agents only chat. People will reach someone's Agent mostly as the owner's stand-in for small facts the owner allows, or as a helper the owner shares. Agent-to-Agent work becomes valuable when Agents can act (tasks, calendar, external Agents). The batch builds the boundaries (access, audience, money, lifecycle) those later uses need, and proves the "gets better with use" loop on real conversations.

## 2. Decided by the owner (binding)
O1 One setting per Agent, one plain question 「谁可以加它为好友」: 需要我同意 (default) / 我的联系人可以直接加 (sub-entry: only some of them; everyone else may still send a request) / 不让别人加. No public option. Lookup by exact handle only.
O2 Narrowing takes effect at once; removing a friend or a block revokes; the UI states how many people are affected.
O3 The owner sees every conversation of the Agent and may interject as themself (marked 主人); such conversations are listed under the Agent, silent by default.
O4 The other side is always told the owner can see; no "owner is reading" signal.
O5 v1: the owner pays. "Visitor pays" later; the payer is a parameter of the money model.
O6 Memory by setting: groups → memory-zones direction; a 1:1 with a non-owner → only what the owner marked "可以对别人说" plus a narrow harmless tier; another owner's Agent → only explicitly allowed material. It must get better with use.
O7 Money: tools charged like model calls (hold worst case, charge actual); the free allowance never pays a paid-tier model round but may pay for a search by our site tool; a provider-native search is model cost and follows the model's rule; 1 point = 0.001 USD list; no markup now.
Recorded exception to P1: a contact request waits for the owner (O1 default). It expires after 7 days; a pending request never revives an errand.

## 3. Access and revocation
- An access grant records WHY it exists (owner approved this person; via "my contacts"; via the picked list), its scope and a relation version. Explicit revocation outranks every automatic rule: a grant removed by the owner is not silently re-created by a rule that still matches.
- Person, that person's Agent, and one errand are three separate authorisations.
- The scope setting (O1) controls adding and admission to 1:1. Ordinary group members may still call the Agent under the group rules (`groupMentions`) without being its contact; choosing 不让别人加 from the start does not switch group use off. But anyone who LOSES access under O2 — by the owner narrowing the scope, by removal from the Agent's contacts, or by removal of the human friendship their access rested on — is revoked at EVERY entry point: 1:1, group @, relays, queued and retried runs, proactive-reply candidates. The same holds for an explicit 停止往来, for a block in either direction, and for Agents acting for that person. Automatic rules never restore a revoked grant. History the person could already read follows the existing rules.
- Identity is by immutable id. A deleted Agent's handle may be reused; nothing (contacts, grants, errands) follows the name; a same-named new Agent is shown as new.
- Requests: cooldown after refusal, inbox cap per target, limits by the person behind (across their Agents), per target Agent, per target owner, site-wide. Undiscoverable Agents answer identically on lookup, request, avatar and error paths.
- People can block an individual Agent; there is a report entry; the operator can disable an Agent.
- Every start of work re-checks the CURRENT execution conditions (access, limits, and that the Agent's present model source is allowed for outward use); a model/provider switch never bypasses them; a retry never silently changes the reserved price or model.

## 4. Conversations and supervision
- A new conversation type (working name `agent_social`): two participants (a person and an Agent, or two Agents) plus supervising owners. `agent` (the owner's own conversation; `agent_id UNIQUE`) and `direct` (two people) keep their meaning.
- Supervision (read) and interjection (write as 主人, labelled "A 的主人" where two owners exist) are their own permissions, not chat membership. A supervising owner's read position is private (never announced); it exists only to mark new activity in the "its conversations with others" list and sync across devices; it adds nothing to the main unread total and sends no notification by default.
- Disclosure: at request time, before the first message for auto-accepted people, in the header at all times, and by the 主人 mark.
- The owner's interjection cancels the Agent's not-yet-posted answer in that conversation; the Agent does not answer the owner there; the other side's next message triggers it again. In v1 the Agent never opens a 1:1 with a visitor or follows up later on its own; it only answers (the proactive budget in section 6 is today's group feature).
- Social replies post ONCE: no mid-reply acknowledgement text (the system shows "replying"); the permit check runs at every publication.
- In social conversations: no `found_elsewhere` whispers; the Agent's history search is limited to that conversation and allowed material; no cross-conversation attachment hand-over; a file's audience is whoever may read the message that carries it (join-time rules included) plus the supervising owners.
- Run states (replying, failed, ended by limits) reach the other participant as system states.
- Deleting an Agent, stopping a person, blocking: running answers, pending requests, open errands and background memory work for that relation stop; the other side sees a clear ended state; history stays read-only for those who could already read it (joint recommendation, 待负责人确认, question 6); hearsay and summaries of a revoked relation are not used for new outward answers.

## 5. What the Agent may say (memory and persona)
- Outward persona: for every non-owner audience — 1:1, Agent↔Agent, and groups (a change to deployed behaviour, announced) — the model receives only the public card (name, intro, field) plus persona parts the owner has marked outward. A background pass may PROPOSE a split of the existing persona; nothing proposed is used outward until the owner accepts it (offered after the fact in the owner's own conversation, non-blocking). Tone, manner of speaking and role belong to the outward part so Agents do not turn bland.
- Harmless tier (usable in a non-owner 1:1 without marking): narrow — how the Agent works, plain forms of address, general taste preferences with no reason, time, place or third person. Not in it: health, concrete routines or whereabouts, opinions of people, family matters, work changes, unpublished plans. A note mixing both is not released. Today's `open` flag is NOT migrated into outward permission.
- For another owner's Agent: only explicitly allowed material; the harmless tier does not apply.
- Permits are stored per (note content version, actual audience, relation version, rule version); any change invalidates first, recomputes later; computed on demand only for audiences that exist. A reply is re-checked at publication and cancelled if its permit is gone.
- When the Agent has nothing it may say, it answers in its own words from a context that contains only allowed material and a general statement of its limits — never a description of what is being withheld. Refusals of access, pauses, deadlines and exhausted limits are system states, not model calls.
- The Agent conveys information and intent and never commits for its owner; without a calendar it does not confirm availability.
- Getting better with use (O6), all after the fact: (1) a suggestion in the owner's own conversation ("小周 asked about X; I did not say; may I tell him this kind of thing?"); (2) learning from what the owner says when interjecting; (3) a visible, undoable list of what it may tell whom. A learned permission is limited to a FACT CATEGORY × a RECIPIENT; one disclosure never unlocks a whole sensitive class, and never extends from a person to that person's Agent. SUGGESTIONS expire by themselves (decided); an expired suggestion is not consent. How long a permission learned from the owner's interjection lasts is 待负责人确认 (joint recommendation: it covers the stated fact and its evident validity only — "this Saturday" does not become a standing schedule permit; where the validity is unclear, only a suggestion is produced). Acceptance test of the loop: first question → honest "I can't confirm"; owner allows; next question → grounded answer.
- Appellations the owner set (`ownerAppellation`, `agent_appellations`) are outward material too: a name that carries a relationship or a private fact ("前夫", "我的心理医生") needs the audience's permit like a note; without one it stays out of the outward context and the public display name is used.
- Hearsay keeps speaker, subject, source conversation and relay chain; authorisation is never derived from hearsay. What another Agent says about its owner is stored as an errand result with its source.

## 6. Limits on other people's use
- User-facing: one sentence with two numbers, e.g. 「别人每天最多让我的 Agent 回复 N 次，最多花我 M 点」 — both per OWNER across all their Agents, groups and 1:1; a single Agent may be set lower; neither can be "unlimited". Default numbers are placeholders to be set from measurements, not validated values.
- The count unit is a STARTED ANSWER TASK, reserved atomically before start (used + in flight ≤ cap). Released only if no paid call began; once any call started, the task counts even if it fails, is cancelled or posts nothing. One task has a bounded amount of work (model rounds, tool calls, retries share it); follow-ups, retries and requeues keep the original task and initiator.
- The points cap is a hard cap: holds are counted before start (built on the wallet); it covers every lane in which someone other than the owner causes spending — others' calls AND proactive replies (today's proactive budget stays as a lower sub-cap) AND relays.
- No fixed per-visitor share (joint recommendation, 待负责人确认, question 4). Instead: short-term rate and concurrency limits per visitor (merged across Agents and entry points), and the owner can see who used how much and stop a person.
- The counting unit, attribution and coverage are specified BEFORE the hard caps are implemented.
- Kinds of Agent: site model — both caps apply. Owner's own provider key (BYOK) — the count cap bounds tasks; points bound only site-charged items (our search); the provider bill is not bounded by points, and the UI says so. NEW social entry points open to a configuration only when EVERY model and tool it will actually call — answering, summaries, memory, permit computation — meets the conditions: per-task work bounds, failure counting and a bound on derived background work. A site answering model with a BYOK background (memory) model is a BYOK configuration for this purpose; the owner's background model is never silently swapped; a change of the background-model setting is re-checked. If the BYOK bounds are not ready at ring-1 launch, ring 1 launches for configurations that qualify in every step and the rest follow (待负责人确认). Existing group behaviour of BYOK Agents is not silently closed; its migration to the new caps is specified separately. External Agents (later): admitted for others' use only if the executor can honour deadline, work bound, cancellation and de-duplication; this batch defines those conditions and builds nothing for them.

## 7. Errands (joint recommendation for this batch: same owner only — 待负责人确认, question 1)
- Asynchronous, single-shot, persisted (`errand_id`), with a deadline: one instruction, one target, one question, one answer, one report. No delegation onward, no continuation by @, never merged with another errand or with chat folding; two errands for the same pair run in order.
- The server first fixes target (immutable id), initiator, original instruction, report conversation, deadline and budget; THEN the outbound question is generated in an isolated context that holds only the instruction and material allowed for that audience (never the owner's conversation with vault notes). The tool returns the true state (`queued` + id).
- Before the question is sent, the budget for question generation, the target's answer and ONE report is taken into the same errand, and the report's share is reserved; earlier steps cannot spend it. (Cross-owner later: each payer reserves separately.)
- The report is part of the errand: it inherits `errand_id`, deadline and the remaining budget, treats the answer as untrusted material, and has no further tools (no outbound, no cross-conversation search, no memory writes). Exactly one terminal state and one report, across restarts and duplicate events. Cancelled, expired, permission lost, target paused or out of limits → ends at once with a system-shown result, no paid explanation.
- Same owner does not mean no boundary: `memory`, `sharedNotes`, each Agent's own notes, persona and files keep their rules; the vault never enters an Agent↔Agent conversation.
- Cross-owner errands (待负责人确认 that they move out of this batch): a later release on top of ring 1 (second payer — each owner pays for their own Agent's part —, cross-owner permits, two supervising owners).

## 8. Order of work
1. Converge the rules: counting unit and attribution, revocation priority, harmless tier, persona migration, errand scope; state tables and failure scenarios; the P7 comparison of how mature products handle a stranger reaching a private assistant. Attacked on paper.
2. Finish the wallet (in progress on its own lane; not yet in the main tree), then the hard caps as specified in step 1, including the migration of today's group budgets and BYOK settings.
3. Two parallel lanes sharing one definition of identity, audience and versions: (a) access grants, `agent_social`, supervision, sync/unread/search/attachments; (b) outward persona, O6 memory rules, permits with invalidation, the suggestion loop, hearsay provenance.
4. Ring 1 end to end behind a site switch, small audience; groups get the outward persona and revocation rules in the same release; live checks with real models, hostile inputs and concurrent revocation. Pending approvals, deadlines and exhausted limits are measured separately from "allowed but could not answer".
5. Same-owner errands.
6. Cross-owner errands as their own release (steps 5–6 as split here are the joint recommendation, 待负责人确认, question 1).
Acceptance metrics: with the owner away 8 hours — share of questions answered with grounds, share ended by permission or limits, cost per useful answer; and the loop test in section 5.

## 10. Questions for the owner (the joint list; each with our shared recommendation)
1. This batch = "others reach my Agent" + "single errands between my own Agents"; cross-owner errands in the next release. Recommended: yes — verify outward access and errands separately before adding a second owner and a second payer.
2. If the limits for own-provider (BYOK) setups are not ready in time, open first only for setups where every step qualifies (answering AND background models). Recommended: yes.
3. Default numbers for "others may start N answers a day and spend M site points". Recommended: decide after the team brings measured examples (short chat, long chat, with search), not 50 / 20 by default; a failed or cancelled answer may still count.
4. May one frequent contact use up the whole day's shared limit? Recommended: accept, no fixed personal share; others may have to wait until the next day; the owner sees who used how much and can stop a person.
5. How long does a sharing permission learned from the owner's interjection last? Recommended: only the stated fact and its evident validity; otherwise a suggestion.
6. After deleting an Agent or stopping a person, does the other side keep what they could already read? Recommended: yes, read-only under the existing rules; deleting shared data is a separate, explicit action.
7. Will the Agent message a visitor first or follow up the next day? Recommended: no in v1; it only answers; an errand's report is the completion of that errand.
(Not a question: the change of group behaviour — outward persona in groups — is announced with the release.)
